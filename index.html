UNDERSTANDING THE ISSUE

What Is HHW?

Everyday products can create extraordinary environmental and health concerns when disposed of improperly.

Household Hazardous Waste

Household hazardous waste (HHW) refers to leftover household products that contain ingredients that may be harmful to human health or the environment if they are not handled or disposed of properly.

These products are commonly found in kitchens, bathrooms, garages, basements, and storage areas. Although they may be used regularly, their chemical properties can make improper disposal dangerous.

Remember: Just because a product is commonly found in your home does not mean it belongs in your regular trash or recycling bin.
KNOW WHAT TO LOOK FOR

Common Household Hazardous Waste

Some examples of household products that may require special disposal include:

🧽

Cleaning Products

Certain household cleaners, disinfectants, and chemical solutions.

🎨

Paint & Solvents

Oil-based paints, paint thinners, and other chemical solvents.

🔋

Batteries

Rechargeable batteries, automotive batteries, and certain other battery types.

🌿

Pesticides

Insecticides, herbicides, and certain lawn and garden chemicals.

🚗

Automotive Products

Motor oil, automotive fluids, and certain automotive chemicals.

💡

Specialty Items

Some fluorescent bulbs, aerosol products, and electronic items.

Not every product in these categories is classified or collected as HHW. Disposal requirements vary by product and location. Always check local guidelines.

WHY PROPER DISPOSAL MATTERS

Where does improperly discarded waste go?

When hazardous household products are disposed of incorrectly, harmful substances can potentially enter the environment.

01

Soil Contamination

Chemicals can enter the soil and affect the surrounding environment.

02

Water Pollution

Some harmful substances can reach groundwater, rivers, and other water sources.

03

Human & Animal Health

Exposure to certain chemicals can create health risks for people and wildlife.

OUR MISSION

Awareness is the first step toward change.

We are students at Columbus North High School working through HOSA – Future Health Professionals to address the issue of improper household hazardous waste disposal in our community.

Through our project, Health Over Hazard, we aim to educate residents about the potential environmental and health consequences of improper disposal while connecting them with accessible local resources.

We believe that meaningful change begins with informed individuals. By making information accessible and encouraging responsible habits, we hope to create a lasting impact throughout Columbus and Bartholomew County.
